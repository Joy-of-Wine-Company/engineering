Coverage vs depth. Coverage = do we return a correct rate for the destination (near-100% counting the conservative state / SST fallback). Depth = do we know it's right — verified, and corroborated by a second source. Depth is the binding constraint.

What each row measures

Legality
Do we carry a direct-to-consumer (DTC) shipping rule for every jurisdiction — is wine shipping legal there, and under what case/volume limits, age check, and permit. This is the gate that runs before any tax question; an order to a prohibited state is blocked regardless of rate.
Tax-rate presence
Does every jurisdiction carry at least one tax-rate record at all — the baseline that we can return a rate for the destination. It says nothing about whether that rate is the finest or most-verified one; just that the jurisdiction isn't a hole.
Verified accuracy
Of the distinct local ZIPs, how many serve a DOR-verified local rate rather than the conservative state-only fallback. "Verified" = correct for ≥99% of a ZIP's residential addresses. The rest fall back to the safe state rate (which never over-collects) — that residue is genuinely address-level-split, and the serve-time rooftop engine (now live) resolves it per-address below the ZIP.
ZIP-exact granularity
Coverage breadth — does each real ZIP carry its own local overlay, or just inherit the bare state rate. The honest figure counts only ZIPs that can carry one — excluding no-local-tax states, non-real placeholder codes, and counties a state's own statute bars from levying (PA grants the local tax to first-class cities and second-class counties only, i.e. Philadelphia and Allegheny — the other 65 counties are state-only as a matter of law). Counting any of them only understates. Orthogonal to accuracy: this asks "does a ZIP have a local row," not "is the rate right."
Independent depth
The binding constraint. Is our local rate corroborated by a SECOND, independent government source — a different agency (LA's LULSTB, AK's DCCED) or a different-product DOR table (OH's Finder, WI's county chart) — so two independent primaries must agree. Coverage says we return a rate; depth says we know it's right.

The scale: each bar is plotted on a reliability-nines axis — 90% = 1 nine, 99% = 2, 99.9% = 3, 99.99% = 4, and the target 99.999% = five nines. Sub-90% figures sit in the leftmost sliver, so a low bar (e.g. independent depth) reflects how far a linear percentage still is from the five-nines target.

What this is. Compliance Vine automates mandatory direct-to-consumer (DTC) wine shipping compliance — the legality rules, permits, volume caps, and state + local tax rates that determine whether an order may ship and what it owes. Wrong data here isn't a bug; it's a merchant's license. So we hold the rules engine's reference data to an infrastructure standard: we measure it the way SREs measure uptime, in nines.

How to read it. Five dimensions, hardest last: does every jurisdiction have a shipping rule, does it have a tax rate, is the served rate verified against the state's Department of Revenue (DOR), does every ZIP carry its own local overlay, and — the binding constraint — is each rate corroborated by a second, independent government source. The climb log below is the unedited engineering record: every gain, every denominator we corrected downward for honesty, and every lever we investigated and retired. The through-line is a data discipline: a rate never ships on one source's word, a fallback never over-collects, and a metric is never improved by redefining it upward.

Why it matters. Legacy compliance vendors treat rate data as a licensed black box. We build and verify ours from primary government sources — statutes, DOR rate tables, Census geography, USPS — with the geocoding stack owned end-to-end. That is the moat: the data itself, and the verification machinery that keeps it honest.
