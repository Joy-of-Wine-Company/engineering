Independent depth

14 of 35 local-depth states now rest on a real second source (LA just joined via the independent LULSTB rate API — the MO/NM geocode bar; before it MN, WI, VT, GA, WY, OH, NC). At 40% — 21 to go, now a per-state geocode-oracle project.

Territories (AS · GU · MP · PR · VI): legality ✅, local depth 0 — but correct by nature. Like the NOMAD states, they carry no local sub-jurisdiction sales tax (PR runs its own IVU regime). The real depth universe is 51 states + DC; the 5 territories are legality-only by design, not a gap.
Coverage vs depth. Coverage = do we return a correct rate for the destination (near-100% counting the conservative state / SST fallback). Depth = do we know it's right — verified, and corroborated by a second source. Depth is the binding constraint.

What each row measures

Excise provenance
Every live wine-excise row names the authority its figure came from, and says whether that authority was actually opened in the pass that set it. A rate nobody can trace is a number every downstream consumer takes on trust. 237/237 cited; 67 rows across 15 states carry a dated primary re-read, 112 rows rest on a single 2026-08-01 audit without the primary being re-opened — and say so in the row — and 41 rows across 24 states gained a citation without the figure being re-derived. Reconciliation against that audit is clean in 47 of 51 states; the four that are not are incidence and scope questions, not rate gaps.
Addresses priced right
The five-nines target stated plainly: of every US address someone could ship wine to, what share is charged the official rate. Each ZIP is weighted by its housing units (Census 2010) and scored by what we serve today: right in no-local-tax states and verified single-rate ZIPs, the measured dominant-rate share in split ZIPs, wrong where a local tax exists but we serve state-only, and unmeasured for the rest, which is why it is a range. Idaho is scored at its 6% state rate: its only local taxes are resort-city taxes the cities collect themselves, and no source shows they reach DTC shippers, so it is state-only pending a legal answer. Address-level resolution earns no credit here until it charges in production.
Legality
Do we carry a direct-to-consumer (DTC) shipping rule for every jurisdiction — is wine shipping legal there, and under what case/volume limits, age check, and permit. This is the gate that runs before any tax question; an order to a prohibited state is blocked regardless of rate.
Tax-rate presence
Does every jurisdiction carry at least one tax-rate record at all — the baseline that we can return a rate for the destination. It says nothing about whether that rate is the finest or most-verified one; just that the jurisdiction isn't a hole.
Verified accuracy
Of the distinct local ZIPs, how many serve a DOR-verified local rate rather than the conservative state-only fallback. "Verified" = correct for ≥99% of a ZIP's residential addresses. The rest fall back to the safe state rate (which never over-collects). A ZIP whose addresses carry more than one rate can never be verified at ZIP level. Address-level resolution prices it per address instead, and it earns its measured address-level accuracy back (CA 99.48% vs CDTFA; IL 97.3% vs IDOR, misses counted wrong) once production charges that rate.
ZIP-exact granularity
Coverage breadth — does each real ZIP carry its own local overlay, or just inherit the bare state rate. The honest figure counts only ZIPs that can carry one — excluding no-local-tax states, non-real placeholder codes, and counties a state's own statute bars from levying (PA grants the local tax to first-class cities and second-class counties only, i.e. Philadelphia and Allegheny — the other 65 counties are state-only as a matter of law). Counting any of them only understates. Orthogonal to accuracy: this asks "does a ZIP have a local row," not "is the rate right."
Independent depth
The binding constraint. Is our local rate corroborated by a SECOND, independent government source — a different agency (LA's LULSTB, AK's DCCED) or a different-product DOR table (OH's Finder, WI's county chart) — so two independent primaries must agree. Coverage says we return a rate; depth says we know it's right.

The scale: each bar shows the plain percentage on a linear 0–100% axis, with the target line at 99.999% (five nines). The nines figure beside each percentage (nines = −log₁₀(1 − fraction)) tells the harder truth a linear bar hides: a bar that looks nearly full at 93.13% is still only 1.16 nines — the last fractions of a percent are where all the work lives.

What this is. Compliance Vine automates mandatory direct-to-consumer (DTC) wine shipping compliance — the legality rules, permits, volume caps, and state + local tax rates that determine whether an order may ship and what it owes. Wrong data here isn't a bug; it's a merchant's license. So we hold the rules engine's reference data to an infrastructure standard: we measure it the way SREs measure uptime, in nines.

How to read it. Six dimensions, hardest last: does every jurisdiction have a shipping rule, does it have a tax rate, is the served rate verified against the state's Department of Revenue (DOR), does every ZIP carry its own local overlay, and — the binding constraint — is each rate corroborated by a second, independent government source. The climb log below is the unedited engineering record: every gain, every denominator we corrected downward for honesty, and every lever we investigated and retired. The through-line is a data discipline: a rate never ships on one source's word, a fallback never over-collects, and a metric is never improved by redefining it upward.

Why it matters. Legacy compliance vendors treat rate data as a licensed black box. We build and verify ours from primary government sources — statutes, DOR rate tables, Census geography, USPS — with the geocoding stack owned end-to-end. That is the moat: the data itself, and the verification machinery that keeps it honest.
